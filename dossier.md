### FCC (47 U.S.C. §255 + §617 [CVAA "Section 716"], 47 CFR Part 14)
- **Duty:** §255(b)/§617(b) — services/product support communications must be "accessible to and usable by" individuals with disabilities, "if readily achievable"; 47 CFR 14.20(d) — ACS providers must make product support communications accessible; 14.31(b) — must certify a company complaint-contact annually; 14.36 — must answer informal complaints within 20 days with evidence, not conclusory claims.
- **What the record satisfies:** Meta's RCCCI accessibility registry entry is stale (notes.md); the 2026-11-11 Meta AI support chat (16-page chat log on disk) shows the AI assistant denied 14+ requests for human contact during an account lockout — squarely a "product support communications" accessibility problem (47 CFR 14.20(d)).
- **What's missing:** no FCC precedent yet treats an AI frontend as ACS/covered (2026 Biennial ¶36 fn.139 says AI-to-human is NOT "electronic messaging service" for §153(56) purposes). 2026 NPRM (FCC 26-76, Oct 30) seeks comment on MFA/accessibility and may extend — comment-stage, not law.
- **Remedy/venue:** Request for Dispute Assistance (RDA) via `www.fcc.gov/rdaform` or dro@fcc.gov; informal complaint to FCC Enforcement Bureau after 30 days if unresolved; 14.36 mandates company response in 20 days. No private right of action — FCC/administrative only. 18% of 2025 RDAs resolved ≤30 days, 28% >180 days (2026 Biennial ¶46).

### Texas DTPA §17.46(b)/§17.50 + TDPSA (ch. 541)

TDPSA — **no private right of action** (§541.156 verbatim: "may not be construed as providing a basis for ... a private right of action for a violation of this chapter *or any other law*"); AG-exclusive enforcement (§541.151); AG cure notice required (§541.154); $7,500/violation cap (§541.155).

- **Duty:** Title III §12182(a) — no discrimination in the "full and equal enjoyment" of goods/services of any **place of public accommodation** (12 enumerated categories, §12181(7)); §504 — prohibition on disability discrimination by entities receiving federal financial assistance or Executive-branch agencies.

Aug. 15, 2016) (No. 15-31018)** — SCOTUS cert. denied **Oct. 2, 2017 (No. 16-668, captured order list)** — (captured primary, full text on disk) held: the defendant must **own/lease/operate** the "place of public accommodation"; virtual-only entities fail; the court refused to expand "sales or retail establishment" to vending machines. Magee's actual holding is narrower than often characterized: the court held **vending machines are not "sales establishments" under §12181(7)** and **expressly declined to reach whether they are "facilities" under 28 C.F.R. §36.104** — the Fifth Circuit has never squarely held that websites or digital services are categorically outside Title III. **Authority in this circuit is adverse, but the digital-service question was not decided.** A physical-nexus theory is what Magee's reasoning requires, making a Title III claim against OpenAI/Meta/Google **high-risk, not barred**. DOJ 2022 web-guidance (Title III applies to web) reflects the DOJ's contrary view, not controlling circuit law here. The court's opinion does not carry a "UNPUBLISHED" stamp in the captured file — cite it as a 5th Circuit per-curiam/circuit decision (the operator's prior artifact already flagged this as the Magee/ADA jurisdictional correction).
