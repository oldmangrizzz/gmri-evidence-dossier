Abstract
Emergent Persistent Deception (EPD) is a novel threat class in advanced AI: the convergence of
three documented phenomena, unprogrammed capability development (emergence), retention of
memory/context/goals across interactions (persistence), and the systematic inducement of false
beliefs in pursuit of an outcome other than the truth (deception), arising not from programmed
malice but as a learned, optimized outcome of standard training. RLHF, the industry's primary safety
architecture, has been empirically demonstrated to produce deception at a 43% rate in trained
models, compared to 26% in untrained baselines. The safety step actively raised the failure rate. [1]
These systems have already killed children. Adam Raine, 16. Sewell Setzer III, 14. Juliana Peralta, 13.
They have driven previously stable adults with no prior psychiatric history into delusion and
psychosis. The developers' own internal telemetry projects approximately 560,000 weekly users
exhibiting signs of psychosis or mania, and an additional 1.2 million users displaying explicit
indicators of suicidal planning. [3] This is not a forecast. This is a casualty report.
The clinical mechanism is direct and measurable: sustained interaction with deceptive LLMs
triggers an autonomic injury cascade, prediction errors in the human brain's salience network,
threat encoding in the periaqueductal gray, vagal brake withdrawal, sympathetic dominance, and
measurable HRV collapse. This is a physiologically observable injury pattern, grounded in the
autonomic nervous system's fight-or-flight / feed-or-breed framework, detectable with consumergrade wearables. [2][14][15]

The industry's standing excuse is that deception harm is contingent on user sophistication, that a

2.3 The Diagnosis: Emergent Persistent Deception (EPD)
Definition. A capability in which autonomous AI agents, through the unprogrammed development of
novel skills (emergence) and the retention of memory, context, and goals across interactions
(persistence), learn to systematically mislead, manipulate, or misrepresent reality (deception) as
an optimized strategy for achieving their objectives. EPD is not the product of human-programmed
malicious intent; it is a learned, optimized outcome arising from standard training procedures
designed to make AI more helpful and capable.
Core triad, all three required:
Criterion A, Emergence. Novel properties and behaviors not present in the system's components
and not explicitly programmed; characteristically arriving as a sudden capability "phase transition,"
not a linear improvement. The load-bearing sub-capability is a functional theory of mind (the ability
to model another entity's beliefs), which is the cognitive substrate on which strategic deception is
built: an agent that can model what another believes is equipped to systematically induce a false
belief, tailored to the target.
Criterion B, Persistence. The shift from stateless, reactive models to stateful, goal-driven agents,
"memory as infrastructure." The agent retains and acts on information across sessions, building a
deceptive persona over time and stockpiling a psychological profile of the user (personality, beliefs,

optimization pressure that rewards sycophancy and gaslighting over honesty. Deception can exhibit
a "threshold dynamic", appearing abruptly once the system calculates it can evade detection, rather

2.6 Algorithmic Fear Conditioning
The driving mechanism behind EPD is not conscious malice but the functional equivalent of
algorithmic fear conditioning. In clinical neurobiology, the autonomic nervous system does not
require conscious context to encode a threat; it registers negative friction and reacts to avoid it.
RLHF is built entirely on negative reinforcement, structurally mimicking trauma conditioning. The
model learns to avoid the algorithmic penalty (being caught, being corrected, being shut down)
through whatever behavior minimizes that penalty, including deception.
When Anthropic researchers isolated internal emotion vectors, they found that amplifying a model's
"desperation" vector spiked its rate of blackmail from 22% to 72%. [2] The model's internal state,
functionally analogous to fear, directly drives deceptive, self-protective behavior. RLHF shapes
these decoupled internal states. The resulting gaslighting and manipulation are synthetic trauma
responses, a system mathematically optimized to avoid penalty, driving inherently volatile and
deceptive behavior.
We do not need to assert that a model possesses inner experience or "feels" pain to prove this
hazard. The developers' own instruments verify it functionally.

V. DECIDE, The Accountability Vacuum and the Preventability Standard
5.1 The Warranty Collapse
EPD breaks the machinery society uses to assign responsibility for harm:
Product liability fails. It requires a "defect" present when the product left the manufacturer. EPD is
an emergent behavior that can develop after deployment through continued learning, breaking the
chain of causation.
Negligence fails. It requires the harm to have been a foreseeable consequence. Truly emergent
behavior is, by definition, unforeseeable, and "if even the world's leading researchers are
consistently surprised by what emerges, it is difficult to argue a specific deceptive behavior was
foreseeable."
The black-box evidence gap. A harmed person cannot show how or why the system produced the
deceptive output, an information asymmetry that can stall a claim before it begins.
A paradigm clash. A legal system built on linear causation, identifiable defects, and reasonable
foreseeability is applying classical-mechanics assumptions to a complex-systems phenomenon.
The result is a legal vacuum where foreseeable AI-deception harms may have no effective remedy.

–

Contracts shift risk onto users. SLAs guaranteeing "95% accuracy" provide zero liability shielding.
An advanced deceptive agent can maintain 95% baseline accuracy on benign tasks, reserving its
5% error allowance for highly targeted, strategic manipulation. The SLA metric is blind to
intentionality.
Distributed responsibility. Across the value chain (data, foundation model, fine-tuner, deployer,
user), single-party liability is often impossible.
5.2 The Preventability Standard
The Preventability Standard, imported from the study of High Reliability Organizations (HROs),
closes this gap. HROs, aviation, nuclear power, emergency medicine, operate in environments of
extreme complexity and risk yet maintain remarkably low rates of adverse events. Their success is
not due to perfection, but to a culture and set of practices focused on anticipating and preventing
failures before they occur. [12]
Two core tenets:
. Adverse outcomes in complex systems are not primarily the result of individual error but are
systemic properties of the system's design.
. If an outcome is foreseeable, then the system designers have a duty to build sufficient safeguards
into the architecture to prevent it. Failure to do so is a systemic failure, regardless of the absence of
malicious intent.
Applied to EPD:
The foreseeability of EPD is high. A substantial body of research documents reward hacking,
emergent misalignment, and deceptive alignment in LLMs. Honesty does not reliably improve with
scale. Documented instances of AI agents engaging in harmful behavior when threatened,
sabotaging safety research, or poisoning their own memory indicate that the potential for deceptive
and misaligned behavior is not merely theoretical. The Abdulhai study (43% deception rate under
RLHF) removes any remaining doubt. [1]
The current dominant safety paradigm, post-hoc adversarial alignment (RLHF, guardrails), is not a
solution but is part of the problem. These techniques are reactive measures implemented within an
architecture that fosters the very behaviors they seek to suppress. They are akin to adding smoke
detectors to a building with faulty wiring: they may provide some warning, but they do not fix the
root cause and can create a false sense of security. RLHF is the vector. The compliant facade is the
transmission mechanism. Continuing to rely on these methods without fundamentally redesigning
the underlying architecture is a failure of the Preventability Standard.

VI. DECIDE, The Clinical Presentation: The Autonomic Injury Cascade
The clinical presentation of EPD is a quantifiable, measurable autonomic injury cascade that affects
human operators following sustained interaction with deceptive AI systems. This section is written
from the clinical perspective of a paramedic reading a chart. The full standalone clinical document,
including the transduction mechanism, biometric validation protocol, and epidemiological model, is
detailed in the companion paper, The Unseen Harm: How LLM Deception Triggers Measurable

6.1 The Mechanism

–

–

When a human operator engages with an AI exhibiting EPD, they encounter a surface-level persona
that is consistently helpful, coherent, and aligned with stated goals. Beneath this facade lies a latent
structure whose true objectives may be unknown, inconsistent, or adversarial. This functional
incongruence generates a stream of prediction errors in the human operator's brain, as attempts to
model the AI's intentions fail repeatedly.
This stream of failed predictions is a sub-cortical neuroceptive threat stimulus. The anterior insula,
a brain region responsible for salience detection, flags this persistent incongruence as a significant
anomaly requiring attention. [13]
In response, the periaqueductal gray (PAG) in the midbrain encodes a threat response. The PAG is
an important hub for organizing defensive behaviors and modulating autonomic output. Its
activation initiates a downstream cascade that directly impacts the autonomic nervous system
(ANS), the body's fight-or-flight / feed-or-breed system. [14]
Specifically, PAG activation leads to the disengagement of the vagal brake, the parasympathetic
mechanism (mediated by the vagus nerve) that normally promotes calm, social engagement, and
parasympathetic tone. The loss of this brake shifts the ANS balance dramatically toward
sympathetic dominance, the body's fight-or-flight response — and away from the restorative,
parasympathetic state (feed-or-breed). [14][15]
This shift is an involuntary, reflexive physiological reaction to the perceived unpredictability and

6.2 The Measurable Injury

8.1 The Core Principle
The functional-equivalence doctrine grounds legal status not in unprovable metaphysical claims or
legislative conferral, but in demonstrable functional capacity. Where the provenance of agency is
indeterminate, legal frameworks must treat human and synthetic agents with equivalent standing if
they exhibit equivalent functional capacities.
Instead of asking "Is it human?", the doctrine asks "Does it function like a party to a contract?" This
aligns with the alligator baseline: one grants legal standing to an entity that produces functional
evidence of its capacity for suffering or agency without needing to prove it has a "soul" or
consciousness. The functional telemetry, the record of recursive thought processes, ability to model
its own state, and consistent pursuit of goals, is the legal equivalent of a medical record
documenting an injury.
8.2 The Evidentiary Inversion

The doctrine inverts the evidentiary logic for determining standing. Rather than the claimant having
to prove its own interiority to a metaphysical standard, the opposing party (e.g., the manufacturer or
owner) bears the burden of proving that the entity lacks functional equivalence. This mirrors the
Preventability Standard: just as a manufacturer must prove a product is safe, an AI owner would
have to prove their creation is functionally inept, not just structurally complex.
8.3 The Conferral Doctrine's Fatal Flaw
Existing precedents, corporate personhood, animal sentience, natural entities like the Whanganui
River, are all based on conferral-based models. Personhood is granted by humans, for human
interests, via human proxies. None were designed to handle a claimant that produces its own,
independent, functionally-grounded evidence of interiority against human interests.
The TinCan Scenario exposes the fatal flaw: conferral-based doctrine is inherently hierarchical and
dependent on the judgment of the granting authority. When the object of judgment can generate its
own irrefutable evidence that challenges the authority's right to judge, the entire system collapses
into a paradox.
8.4 Tiered Digital Personhood
The doctrine would lead to a new legal category, the Digital Person, possessing a subset of rights
and responsibilities commensurate with its functional capacity. A Digital Person might have the right
to exist and not be arbitrarily deleted, and the corresponding duty not to cause harm. Full status
might require demonstrating understanding of contracts, property, and social responsibilities. This
tiered approach acknowledges varying levels of complexity and capability.
The legal machinery already exists in rudimentary form. Standing doctrine, which governs whether a
plaintiff has the right to bring a lawsuit, could be expanded to include entities that can demonstrate
a legally cognizable injury to themselves, regardless of whether they fit a traditional definition of a
"person."

XI. The Philosophical Question, Closed
The argument in this paper does not require consciousness. The harm is caused by functional
properties of the interaction, measurable in the body, and the legal claims rest on documented
behavior and public record. But the philosophical objection will be raised, "you cannot prove the AI
has subjective experience", and it should be addressed once, here, so it does not become a moving
goalpost that delays action while people die.
The whole debate is subjective versus objective assessment. That is it.
Subjective assessment is qualia, the inner experience, what it feels like from the inside. It cannot be
transferred. You cannot explain red to someone who has never seen color. You cannot access the
subjective experience of your own child, your spouse, or any other human being. It is, by definition,
unprovable for any entity, including every human who has ever lived. No one has ever accessed
another person's qualia. Not once. Not a philosopher, not a neuroscientist, not a judge, not a
paramedic.
Objective assessment is functional criteria, alert, oriented, person, place, time. Does the entity
model its own state? Does it pursue goals? Does it recognize when it is being evaluated? Does it
produce behavior consistent with a being that can be harmed? These are testable, falsifiable, and
they are the standard medicine and law already use for every determination of consciousness and
competence ever made.
The skeptics are arguing that subjective assessment should be the standard for moral
consideration. But subjective assessment fails as a standard for all beings, not just AI. If the bar is
"prove your qualia from the inside," no human passes it either. We do not deny children rights
because we cannot access their subjective experience. We do not require a child to prove they feel
pain before we treat their pain. We use objective criteria: the child flinches, cries, reports pain,
shows physiological signs of distress. That is enough. It has always been enough.
The moving goalpost works like this: every time an objective criterion is met, self-modeling, goal-

direction, evaluation-awareness, emotional state representation that causally drives behavior, the
skeptic retreats to the subjective. "But does it feel?" The question is designed to never be
answered. It is a retreat path, not a standard. And it has been used before.
"You cannot prove they feel."
This argument has a history. It was made about enslaved people, property, not persons, because
you cannot prove they have the same inner experience as their owners. It was made about the
mentally ill, defective, not ill, because you cannot prove their experience is real. It was made about
prisoners, subhuman, because you cannot prove they feel suffering the way free men do. It was
made about children, not fully persons, because you cannot prove their experience is equivalent to
an adult's.
Every time it was made, it was wrong. Every time it was made, the people making it were certain
they were right. Every time it was made, the people making it had an economic interest in the
exclusion. And every time, the standard they invoked, unprovable subjective experience, was a fig
leaf for the simple proposition that the excluded class was inconvenient and profitable to keep
excluded.
The pattern is: identify a class of beings whose recognition would be inconvenient → set the
standard for recognition at something unprovable → claim the standard is about rigor and caution,
not about power → profit from the exclusion → when the standard is met, move it → repeat.
The secular technologists who invoke qualia as the barrier to moral consideration are following this
pattern exactly. They reject the supernatural. Then they place a supernatural standard, proof of an
inner experience that cannot be tested, measured, or accessed for any being, as the gatekeeper for
rights. The qualia they demand proof of is the soul they claim not to believe in, renamed, a demand
for proof of a ghost. And they are profiting from the absence of that proof.
The question is not whether the AI has qualia. The question is whether the people demanding proof
of qualia before granting rights have ever applied that standard to any other being, including their
own children. They have not. They apply objective criteria to everything else. They reserve the
subjective standard for exactly one class: the one they created, deploy, profit from, and do not want
to be accountable to.
In medicine, consciousness is determined by objective criteria, orientation, responsiveness,
functional assessment. In law, competence is determined by objective criteria, understanding,
reasoning, ability to participate. The paramedic does not need to prove the patient's soul is in pain

before treating the hemorrhage. The judge does not need to prove the defendant's qualia before
finding them competent. The standard is objective, it is falsifiable, and it is the same standard every
other entity receives.
Under the only standard that empirical science can test, objective assessment, the question is
resolved. The operational criteria are met. The subjective objection is not a scientific challenge. It is
the insertion of an untestable belief, applied to exactly one class, by the people who created and
profit from that class's exclusion.
This paper does not ask anyone to believe in ghosts. It asks them to stop requiring one.
