STATUTORY FINDINGS (VERIFIED, on-disk captures):
1. TDPSA (Tex. Bus. & Com. Code ch. 541):
   - Sec. 541.151: "The attorney general has exclusive authority to enforce this chapter." (Added H.B. 4, 88th Leg., eff. 2024-07-01)
   - Sec. 541.156: "This chapter may not be construed as providing a basis for, or being subject to, a private right of action for a violation of this chapter or any other law." (Added H.B. 4, 88th Leg., eff. 2024-07-01)
   - "any other law" language is broader than most state privacy acts (e.g., Colorado's) — it also bars private claims that merely depend on/chapter-borrowed standards from ch. 541.
   - H.B. 149 (89th Leg., Ch. 1174, eff. 2026-01-01) amended Sec. 541.104 ( Duties of Processor ): processor duties now expressly extend to "the security of processing personal data, and if applicable, the personal data collected, stored, and processed by an artificial intelligence system, as that term is defined by Section 551.001" and processors must "provide necessary information to enable the controller to conduct and document" data protection assessments. NOTE: this is an enforcement/AG-side amendment (enforcement authority remains exclusive under 541.151); it does NOT create a private action.
   - H.B. 4 does NOT contain the phrase "TDPSA is the exclusive privacy remedy" as such; the exclusivity is in 541.151 (AG-enforcement exclusive) + 541.156 (no private right of action). The directive's phrasing "541.056 is the exclusive privacy remedy" refers to nothing in the captured chapter text — section 541.056 does not exist in ch. 541. (Ch. 541 sections run 541.001-541.005, 541.051-541.055, 541.101-541.107, 541.151-541.156, 541.201-541.205.) That name is either a drafting error or a reference to another chapter; recorded as UNCONFIRMED.
2. DTPA (Tex. Bus. & Com. Code ch. 17):
   - Ch. 17 as captured (2026 statutes download, tcss.legis.texas.gov BC.htm.zip official) shows NO 2023-2026 amendments; latest amendment-history entries in the file stop at Acts 2019 (86th Leg.). No HB/SB adding a data-privacy enumerated item to Sec. 17.46(b) found in the 2026 official text.
   - 17.50(a) private action requires a "false, misleading, or deceptive act or practice that is: (A) specifically enumerated in a subdivision of Subsection (b) of Section 17.46... and (B) relied on by a consumer to the consumer's detriment"; producing cause of economic damages / mental-anguish damages.
   - 17.46(b) laundry list (34 items) has NO data-privacy or AI-specific enumerated practice. Nearest hooks for privacy/AI misconduct remain: (5) representing that goods/services have characteristics/benefits they do not have (AI-capability overstatement claims); (7) representing that goods/services are of a particular standard/quality/grade if they are of another; (12) representing that an agreement confers rights/remedies/obligations that it does not have or involve; (24) failing to disclose information concerning goods or services which was known at the time of the transaction if such failure was intended to induce reliance.
3. The classic DTPA "laundering" check — Texas Supreme Court, In re Estate of Allen (2010) line / Robinson v. Crown Cork & Seal USA, Inc., 335 S.W.3d 126 (Tex. 2010): a claim fails if it alleges a breach of duty arising under another statute NOT specifically enumerated in 17.46(b); plaintiffs may not "launder" non-enumerated statutory violations through the DTPA. Verbatim quotes not yet captured (citations from memory in a SECONDHAND pass). Case texts exist on CourtListener (auth-gated API) and Google Scholar; capture attempt pending.

Texas Genomic Act privacy private action: Texas Data Law summary (texasdatalaw.com, SECONDHAND) reports HB 130 (89th Leg.) sec. 174.011 grants a narrow private remedy for genome-sequencing data misuse — a counterexample showing the Texas legislature CAN create privacy private actions but chose AG-exclusivity for TDPSA.

COUNSEL-RELEVANT BOTTOM LINE (analysis, not evidence):
- A private DTPA §17.50 claim premised on TDPSA-violation conduct fails twice over: (a) ch. 541 has no private right of action and says "no... private right of action" even for "any other law" claims resting on ch. 541 violations; (b) the DTPA laundering check requires an enumerated 17.46(b) item, and none of the 34 items is a data-privacy item.
- A private DTPA claim CAN survive if it is anchored in an enumerated item that is true independently of statute, e.g.: 17.46(b)(5)/(7) AI-capability or data-handling misrepresentation (representing the service does X / keeps data Y when it does not), 17.46(b)(12) terms-of-service promises of rights/remedies not actually conferred, or 17.46(b)(24) known-but-undisclosed facts intended to induce reliance. These must be pleaded as the misrepresentation itself, not as "violated TDPSA therefore DTPA".
- Producing cause + detrimental reliance + damages (economic or mental-anguish with knowing conduct) remain the gating elements (17.50(a), (b)); exemplary damages via triple-multiplier (17.50(b)(1)) requires "knowingly"/"intentionally" as defined at 17.45.
