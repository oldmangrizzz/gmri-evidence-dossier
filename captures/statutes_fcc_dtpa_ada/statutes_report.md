### 1. FCC / Communications Act

**47 U.S.C. § 255** (row 465, LII capture): definitions borrow ADA § 12102(2)(A) ("disability") and § 12181(9) ("readily achievable"). Operative duties, verbatim: § 255(b) — "A manufacturer of telecommunications equipment or customer premises equipment **shall ensure** that the equipment is designed, developed, and fabricated to be accessible to and usable by individuals with disabilities, **if readily achievable**." § 255(c) — "A provider of telecommunications service shall ensure that the service is accessible to and usable by individuals with disabilities, if readily achievable." § 255(d) — compatibility with peripheral devices/specialized CPE when (b)/(c) are not readily achievable.

**47 U.S.C. § 617 — the CVAA "Section 716"** (rows 468 + codification evidence in rows 477/478):

- CODIFICATION FINDING (VERIFIED): "Section 716" of the Communications Act is codified at **47 U.S.C. § 617**. Evidence on disk: (i) Cornell 404s on /uscode/text/47/716 (access row B1) and serves § 617 "Access to advanced communications services and equipment"; (ii) GPO USCODE-2023-title47.htm source credit "(June 19, 1934, ch. 652, title VII, §716, as added Pub. L. 111–260, title I, §104(a), Oct. 8, 2010, 124 Stat. 2755…)"; (iii) Statutes at Large 124 Stat. 2751 (row 478): CVAA SEC. 104 adds Comm. Act "‘‘SEC. 716''" with OFR side-note "**47 USC 617**". FCC's own DA-26-1083 (row 452) quotes: "Section 716 requires ACS providers and equipment manufacturers to make their services and equipment accessible to and usable by people with disabilities, unless doing so is 'not achievable.'" Cite as: 47 U.S.C. § 617 [Comm. Act § 716].
- Operative text (LII § 617 verbatim): "a manufacturer of equipment used for advanced communications services, including end user equipment, network equipment, and software, **shall ensure** that the equipment and software … shall be accessible to and usable by individuals with disabilities, **unless the requirements of this subsection are not achievable**" (§ 617(a)(1)); parallel duty for ACS providers (§ 617(a)(2)). Scope per FCC: interconnected/non-interconnected VoIP, electronic messaging, interoperable video conferencing, and equipment used with them (DA-26-1083).

**47 U.S.C. § 618** (row 469): Commission must establish rules "that facilitate the filing of formal and informal complaints that allege a violation of section 255, 617, or 619," enforcement procedures, and recordkeeping; "The Commission shall not charge any fee to an individual."

**47 CFR Part 14** (row 470, eCFR versioner XML at issue date 2026-11-19; LII mirrors rows B7):

- **§ 14.20(d) — product support communications duty:** "Manufacturers and service providers must ensure that the information and documentation that they provide to customers is accessible, if achievable. Such information and documentation includes, but is not limited to, user guides, bills, installation guides for end user devices, and **product support communications**. The requirement to ensure the information is accessible also includes ensuring that individuals with disabilities can access, at no extra cost, call centers and customer support regarding both the product generally and the accessibility features of the product."
- **§ 14.31(b) — complaint-contact certification:** "An officer of each manufacturer and service provider subject to section 255, 716, or 718 of the Act, must sign and file an annual compliance certificate with the Commission" identifying "the name and contact details of the person or persons within the company that are authorized to resolve complaints alleging violations of our accessibility rules," supported by an officer's affidavit/declaration under penalty of perjury, filed annually since April 1, 2013, and updated to keep contact information current. § 14.32(c): "CGB shall serve the manufacturer or service provider using the contact details of the certification … Service using contact details provided pursuant to § 14.31(b) is deemed served. **Failure … to file or keep the contact information current will not be a defense of lack of service.**"
- **§ 14.36 — informal complaint answer duty:** "(a) After a complainant makes a prima facie case … the manufacturer or service provider … **bears the burden of proving** that the product or service is accessible or … that accessibility is not achievable … **Conclusory and unsupported claims are insufficient to carry this burden of proof.**" "(b) … **shall file and serve an answer** … within **twenty days** of service … Respond specifically to each material allegation … **declaration by an officer** … (iv) Set forth any remedial actions already taken or proposed alternative relief…"; "(c) … shall serve the complainant and the Commission with a **non-confidential summary of the answer** … within twenty days"; "(d) The complainant may file and serve a reply … within ten days."
- **§ 14.37:** Commission order "within **180 days**" finding violation or not.
- Path prerequisites: § 14.32 dispute assistance (mandatory; informal complaint requires certification of the § 14.32 Request for Dispute Assistance ≥30 days prior, § 14.34(b)(5)); § 14.21 performance objectives (operable without vision/hearing/speech, limited cognition etc.); § 14.2 conduit/index limitation (scope defense).

### 2. Texas DTPA (Bus. & Com. Code ch. 17, subch. E) — rows 471/479

- § 17.41: "This subchapter may be cited as the Deceptive Trade Practices-Consumer Protection Act."
- § 17.45(4): "'Consumer' means an individual … who seeks or acquires by purchase or lease, any goods or services" (minus the $25M business carve-out); (2) "Services" = "work, labor, or service purchased or leased for use."
- § 17.46(a): "False, misleading, or deceptive acts or practices in the conduct of any trade or commerce are hereby declared unlawful…"; § 17.46(b)(5): "representing that goods or services have sponsorship, approval, characteristics, ingredients, uses, benefits, or quantities which they do not have…"
- § 17.50(a): consumer may sue where enumerated § 17.46(b) act is a "producing cause of economic damages or damages for mental anguish" and was "relied on by a consumer to the consumer's detriment" (also breach of warranty; unconscionable action).
- § 17.50(b): economic damages; if **knowing**, + mental-anguish damages and treble of economic damages; if **intentional**, treble of combined mental-anguish + economic damages; injunction; restitution; other relief as court deems proper.
- § 17.50(d): "Each consumer who prevails shall be awarded court costs and reasonable and necessary attorneys' fees."
- § 17.565 LIMITATION: "All actions brought under this subchapter must be commenced **within two years** after the date on which the false, misleading, or deceptive act or practice occurred or within two years after the consumer discovered or in the exercise of reasonable diligence should have discovered the occurrence…" (+180 days if defendant knowingly induced delay).
- § 17.42 waivers void (written + counsel-signed exception); § 17.49 media exemption.

### 3. Texas TDPSA (ch. 541) — rows 472/480 (Acts 2023, 88th Leg., Ch. 995 (H.B. 4), eff. July 1, 2024)

- § 541.051(b): controller shall comply with an authenticated request to (1) confirm/access; (2) **correct inaccuracies**; (3) **delete personal data**; (4) portable copy; (5) opt out of targeted advertising, sale, or profiling with legal effects.
- § 541.052(b): respond "without undue delay," "not later than the 45th day after the date of receipt" (one 45-day extension w/ notice); (c) if declining, give justification + appeal instructions by the same 45-day clock; (d) info free of charge, twice annually.
- § 541.055(a): "A controller shall establish **two or more secure and reliable methods**" for consumer requests; may not require a new account.
- § 541.053: appeal process; written outcome within 60 days of the appeal.
- § 541.151: "The attorney general has **exclusive authority** to enforce this chapter." § 541.152: AG must run a consumer complaint mechanism. § 541.153: CIDs.
- § 541.154: AG 30-day notice + cure window before suing; cure + written statement (cured; consumer notified) forecloses action.
- § 541.155(a): post-cure violation → civil penalty "**not to exceed $7,500 for each violation**"; AG may seek penalties, injunction, fees/expenses.
- **NUMBERING CORRECTION (VERIFIED negative):** the chapter contains **no § 541.056 and no § 541.058** (sections present: 541.001–.005, 541.051–.055, 541.101–.107, 541.151–.156, 541.201–.205). AG enforcement is §§ 541.151–.155; the only consumer-side recourse beyond the process itself is § 541.053 appeal — **no private right of action** under TDPSA.

### 4. ADA Title III + Section 504 (rows 475/476/473)

- § 12182(a) verbatim: "No individual shall be discriminated against on the basis of disability in the **full and equal enjoyment** of the goods, services, facilities, privileges, advantages, or accommodations of any place of public accommodation by any person who owns, leases (or leases to), or operates a place of public accommodation."
- § 12181(7): public accommodation = exhaustive list of commercial establishments (inn/hotel; restaurant; theater; sales or rental establishment; service establishment; etc.). Applying § 12182 to a web-only AI service: unsettled circuit split; DOJ guidance reads the online offering of covered goods/services as covered (below), but for Title III the DOJ has by rule adopted no technical standard.
- § 12182(b)(2)(A)(ii–iii): auxiliary aids/services where necessary unless "fundamentally alter the nature of the good or service" or "undue burden."
- 29 U.S.C. § 794 verbatim: "No otherwise qualified individual with a disability … shall, solely by reason of her or his disability, be excluded … under **any program or activity receiving Federal financial assistance** or under **any program or activity conducted by any Executive agency** or by the USPS." Inapplicable to a purely private vendor absent federal funding/executive-agency program status.

### 5. DOJ web guidance (row 474)

Captured ada.gov/resources/web-guidance/ ("Guidance on Web Accessibility and the ADA," page dateModified 2026-07-29; still displayed). Verbatim: "Title III prohibits discrimination against people with disabilities by businesses open to the public (also referred to as 'public accommodations' under the ADA)." Businesses must provide "full and equal enjoyment" and "must take steps to provide appropriate communication aids and services … where necessary"; even with flexibility, "they still must ensure that the programs, services, and goods that they provide to the public — **including those provided online** — are accessible to people with disabilities"; WCAG and Section 508 Standards cited as "helpful guidance." Supersession note captured: guidance "does not reflect requirements for state and local governments that were published in the Federal Register on April 24, 2024" (2024 rule governs **Title II**; Title III status unchanged — guidance + § 12182 operative). The pre-existing local file `adagov_sec_compliance_guide.html` is the **Title II** small-entity guide, not this guidance.

### Integration cautions

1. Cite ACS accessibility as **47 U.S.C. § 617 [Comm. Act § 716]**; the /47/716 Cornell URL does not exist.
2. TDPSA: AG-only enforcement (§ 541.151), cure procedure (§ 541.154), $7,500/violation (§ 541.155(a)); no private right of action — any "541.056/541.058" reference must be corrected before filing.
3. DTPA: treble structure (knowing vs. intentional), 2-year SOL (§ 17.565), automatic fees (§ 17.50(d)), reliance + producing cause required for § 17.46(b) claims.
4. ADA Title III: § 12181(7) categories are establishments; web-only service coverage is a litigated question; DOJ guidance supports online-goods/services duty but is not an adopted Title III standard; § 794 needs federal nexus.
