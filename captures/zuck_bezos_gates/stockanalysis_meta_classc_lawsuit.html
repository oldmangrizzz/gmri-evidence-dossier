Meta pauses Mark Zuckerberg's Class C share plan while investors' lawsuit against it is fought

Editor: Kris Gunnars · Nov 19, 2026 3:45 AM ET

Meta Platforms (META) agreed in a Delaware court filing on Wednesday not to issue any of its new non-voting Class C shares while shareholders' lawsuit against the plan goes on.

The same filing proposes a trial in the Court of Chancery from April 19 to 23, 2027. The schedule still needs the approval of Chancellor Kathaleen St. J. McCormick.

The pause covers the shares, not the charter that creates them. Meta's new charter can still take effect on January 19, as planned when the company announced the Class C stock with its Q3 results.

Under the original terms, holders were to get one Class C share for each Class A or Class B share within five business days of that date.

Meta wants the shares as a currency for financings, acquisitions and employee pay, so it can fund AI spending with stock. Because they carry no votes, Mark Zuckerberg could also sell some of his without losing majority control. They would have helped him pay California's billionaire tax, which voters rejected this month.

Shareholders filed 10 lawsuits accusing Zuckerberg and other directors of breaching their fiduciary duties. On November 6, the court merged them into one case.

The plaintiffs' lawyers argued in one complaint:"Public stockholders receive a pro rata stock dividend, but no corresponding governance protection or vote on the transaction."

Meta, which had asked for a faster trial than the plaintiffs wanted, said in a statement: "In the best interest of our shareholders, we are seeking a final determination as quickly as the court will allow, and we will vigorously defend against these claims."

Meta and the other defendants must answer the complaint by November 24. If a final ruling voids the new charter, Meta has agreed to rescind it, along with the mandatory convertible preferred stock it sold in October.
