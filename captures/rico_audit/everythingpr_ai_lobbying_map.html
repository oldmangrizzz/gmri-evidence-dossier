AI Lobbying Spend 2026: Anthropic $5.6M, Meta Leads

Anthropic spent a record $2.11 million on federal lobbying in Q3 2026 and OpenAI spent a record $1.53 million, according to lobbying reports signed October 20. Anthropic's roughly $5.6 million through three quarters already exceeds the $3.1 million it spent in all of 2025, and OpenAI's nearly $3.8 million is well ahead of its 2025 pace. Meta led all companies in 2025 with $26.29 million, and more than 3,570 federal lobbyists worked AI issues that year, roughly one in four.

EPR Research released The AI Lobbying Industry Map 2026, the first systematic mapping of how AI companies and their trade associations are deploying federal lobbying capacity. The Study draws from the Senate Office of Public Records, the House Lobbying Disclosure database, OpenSecrets, Issue One, and Public Citizen's analysis of LDA filings.

What Did AI Companies Spend on Federal Lobbying in Q3 2026?

Federal disclosures filed in October show Anthropic, OpenAI and Nvidia each reporting their highest quarterly lobbying total on record. Meta again reported the largest quarterly total of the AI companies tracked, at about $5.9 million, roughly flat against Q2.

CompanyQ2 2026Q3 2026Note
Anthropic$1.97M$2.11MRecord; about $5.6M through Q3
OpenAI$1.2M$1.53MRecord; nearly $3.8M through Q3
Nvidia$1.25M$2.15MRecord, up about 72%; $4.7M through Q3
Meta$5.99MAbout $5.9MLargest of the companies tracked

The totals are each company's own in-house filing and do not include outside lobbying firms, which file separately. The companies also report under different methods: OpenAI and Anthropic use Lobbying Disclosure Act definitions, while Nvidia reports under Internal Revenue Code section 162(e), so the figures are not strictly comparable.

The filings name issues and bills but not positions. Anthropic's disclosure lists the Collaboration on Adversarial Threats and Security Risks Act, which would let AI labs coordinate on security risks, along with child-safety and export-control legislation. OpenAI's lists AI security cooperation, information sharing and antitrust protections. Nvidia's references the Chip Security Act and the Remote Access Security Act. Bloomberg Government reported that the National Association of Realtors displaced the U.S. Chamber of Commerce as the top federal lobbying spender through the third quarter, while AI companies posted some of the fastest growth.

What Did AI Companies Spend on Federal Lobbying in Q2 2026?

Federal disclosures filed in July showed both leading AI labs raising their lobbying outlays for a second straight quarter, ahead of the midterm elections. Per CNBC's review of the filings and Bloomberg Government:

CompanyQ1 2026Q2 2026Change
Anthropic$1.56M$1.97MUp 26%
OpenAI$1.02M$1.2MUp about 18%
Anthropic and OpenAI combined$2.58M$3.17MUp 23%
MetaAbout $7.0M$5.99MDown 15%

Both labs roughly doubled their lobbying outlays compared with Q2 2025. Anthropic, OpenAI and Waymo together spent a record $4.3 million in the quarter, per Bloomberg Government. Meta, Amazon, Google, Microsoft and Nvidia together reported about $21.25 million, nearly flat against Q1. Filings list cybersecurity, copyright, cloud computing and defense procurement among the issues the AI labs lobbied on.

Why Anthropic Outspent OpenAI in Q1 2026

Per Issue One's review of newly filed federal lobbying reports, 11 key technology, social media, and AI companies and their associated trade associations together spent a combined $20 million on federal lobbying between January and March 2026, an average of about $226,000 per day.

Anthropic reported nearly $1.6 million in Q1 2026, a 333 percent increase from the $360,000 it spent in Q1 2025, an average of roughly $17,000 per day. OpenAI reported $1 million for the quarter, an 82 percent year-over-year increase. Nvidia reported $1.3 million in Q1 2026, up 37 percent year-over-year. Snap reported $400,000 and X reported $190,000, both up modestly. ByteDance, parent of TikTok, went the other direction: $1.6 million in Q1 2026, down roughly 45 percent from $2.8 million in Q1 2025, following the January 2026 deal that restructured U.S. ownership through Oracle, Silver Lake, and MGX. ByteDance and TikTok USDS reported $1.88 million combined in Q2 2026, per Issue One's later update.

How Many Lobbyists the Largest AI Companies Employ in Washington

According to Issue One, the six largest tech, social media, and AI companies (Alphabet, Anthropic, Meta, Microsoft, Nvidia, and OpenAI) collectively hired 307 lobbyists during Q1 2026. That is the equivalent of slightly more than one lobbyist for every two members of Congress.

  • Alphabet alone hired 88 lobbyists, roughly one for every six members of Congress.

Per Public Citizen's analysis of House lobbying disclosure data, 3,570 federal lobbyists reported lobbying on AI issues at least once in 2025, more than 25 percent of the entire federal lobbying corps. Three years ago, that figure was 11 percent. The number of unique lobbyist-client relationships on AI issues has grown 265 percent over the past three years. The number of data center lobbyists alone has grown nearly 500 percent.

If AI were classified as a single OpenSecrets issue area, it would rank fourth in the United States behind only the federal budget and appropriations, taxes, and health. For the broader spending picture, see Biggest Lobbying Groups in the US.

What Each Company's Filings Reveal About Its Policy Focus

Anthropic has pivoted into healthcare AI and Defense Department procurement. Q1 2026 disclosures reference the Healthcare AI Accountability Act (S. 4178) and DoD acquisition policy. Anthropic retained Ballard Partners to pursue Pentagon AI procurement contracts following public disputes over Anthropic's acceptable use policy and classified-setting deployment.

OpenAI has pivoted into energy and infrastructure, focused on the Stargate joint venture with SoftBank and Oracle and on data center permitting reform, including the Data Center Energy Efficiency Act (H.R. 4425). OpenAI's lobbying spend has grown roughly 7x year-over-year on this push.

Meta has the most aggressive state-level operation. Per CalMatters, Meta spent at least $4.6 million lobbying California state officials in 2025, more than in any prior year since it began Sacramento advocacy in 2010, and transferred $20 million to a new political committee created to support candidates favoring fewer AI regulations. Meta also made 34 behested payments totaling $1,245,000 at the request of California officials in 2025.

The Revolving Door Between AI Companies and Washington

OpenAI hired Reginald Babin, former legal counsel to then-Senate Majority Leader Chuck Schumer, as a registered lobbyist. OpenAI later hired veteran political operative Chris Lehane as VP of policy and global head of policy. Meta recruited a former Trump adviser as president and vice chairman in 2025. OpenAI hired a former Trump adviser to lead its global energy policy. OpenAI President Greg Brockman and his wife together gave $25 million to a Trump-aligned political action committee.

Anthropic brought on its first in-house lobbyist, Department of Justice alum Rachel Appleton, in late 2024. The Data Center Coalition, which counts OpenAI and Anthropic among its members, reported $420,000 in Q1 2026 lobbying, more than tripling from $123,000 in Q1 2025.

How This Study Was Compiled

The Study draws from Senate Office of Public Records LDA filings (LD-1 registrations and LD-2 quarterly reports), House lobbying disclosure data, OpenSecrets aggregations, Issue One filings reviews, Public Citizen analysis of AI-issue lobbying volume, Bloomberg compilations of company-level totals, CNBC and Bloomberg Government reporting on Q2 and Q3 2026 filings, and CalMatters analysis of California state lobbying. Coverage spans federal lobbying for FY2023 through Q3 2026, plus California state lobbying for 2024 and 2025. Aligned with the six-component EPR Research architecture.

Disclosed Limits

Lobbying disclosure undercounts: not all advocacy meets the LDA registration threshold, dark-money advocacy is excluded entirely, and the February 2025 DOJ memorandum from Attorney General Pam Bondi limiting FARA enforcement may suppress future filings. Quarterly figures are based on initial filings and may be revised. Company totals cover in-house filings and different companies use different reporting methods, as noted above.

Why This Lobbying Surge Matters for Communications Teams

For 25 years, the most consequential algorithm in communications was Google PageRank. The most consequential algorithm in U.S. federal policymaking is now whichever AI model becomes the dominant inference engine inside government. The companies fighting to set those rules are deploying lobbyists at the highest rate in the history of federal disclosure. Every public affairs firm, every comms team at a regulated company, every reporter covering AI policy, and every state attorney general's office has reason to track this map. Frequently Asked Questions

How much did Anthropic and OpenAI spend on lobbying in Q3 2026?

Anthropic reported $2.11 million and OpenAI reported $1.53 million for the third quarter of 2026, each a record, up from $1.97 million and $1.2 million in Q2. Their reports were signed October 20.

Which AI company spends the most on federal lobbying?

Meta, by a wide margin. It reported $26.29 million in 2025, the most of any company in any industry, and about $5.9 million in Q3 2026.

How much did Nvidia spend on lobbying in Q3 2026?

Nvidia reported about $2.15 million, a record and about 72% more than the $1.25 million it reported in Q2. It reports under a different method from OpenAI and Anthropic.

How many lobbyists work on AI issues?

3,570 federal lobbyists reported lobbying on AI issues at least once in 2025, more than 25 percent of the federal lobbying corps, per Public Citizen.
