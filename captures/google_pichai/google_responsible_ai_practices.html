When Android Apps Request Location with a Reason: A Developer Study and User Behavior Experiment
Alex Berke
May Smith
Aman Jain
Mihai Christodorescu
Proceedings on Privacy Enhancing Technologies (2027)
Preview abstract Recent reports have highlighted how mobile apps share user location data with third parties, risking user privacy and platform trust. Although location data is highly sensitive, when users grant apps location access, they may not know the full extent to which it is used. We study how requiring Android apps to show a reason for location access could impact developers, users, and the platform. We surveyed 323 Android app developers and found most supported such a requirement. The majority said it would have a positive impact on user privacy, trust for apps, and trust for Android, where impact on user trust for Android correlated most strongly with support. Many developers also said the intervention would increase the number of users granting location access. Yet their open-ended comments also revealed consistent concerns, such as apps providing dishonest reasons and platform verification. To study the impact on user behavior, we conducted a randomized controlled experiment with 2579 US Android users. We tested how users' decisions to grant location access were impacted by app type, whether reasons were included in the requests, and the content of the reasons, including monetization. We did not find the reasons impacted users' decisions; decisions were instead driven by app type and demographics. Yet we did find the reasons could have a positive impact on user perception for the platform when the reasons did not include using data for ads. Our findings provide insights into developers' willingness to implement privacy-enhancing changes, and expose limits to improving user privacy by simply adding information to user interfaces. A Data-Driven Biophotonic Skin Model for Wearable Sensing
Kaiyuan (Kai) Yao
Hamed Vavadi
Biomedical Optics Express (2027) (to appear)
Preview abstract Accurate modeling of light-matter interactions in biological tissue is fundamental to the advancement of biophotonic sensing, in-vivo diagnostics, and the development of next-generation wearable health monitors. While computational methods such as Monte Carlo simulations are powerful tools for virtual prototyping, the reliability of their predictions is strictly limited by the fidelity of the underlying skin model. Current industry-standard models primarily rely on optical parameters derived from ex-vivo tissue samples or dataset with limited size and variabilities in measurement methods, often failing to capture the complex spectral responses and inter-subject variability found in living populations. To address this limitation, we present a data-driven biophotonic skin model rigorously calibrated and validated against extensive in-vivo dataset. Our study utilizes smartwatch data acquired across a broad spectral range (UV to SWIR) and multiple sensor geometries, with subjects covering a diverse range of skin tones. By grounding the standard multilayer skin architecture in experimental data, this work provides a robust, statistically validated foundation for the design, simulation, and optimization of future wearable optical sensors. Who’s Doing the Thinking Now? Reflexivity and Oversight in GenAI-Mediated Decision-Making
Glorin Sebastian
Oliver Herrbach
Siddharth Gaurav Majhi
Under review with Information Systems Journal (2027) (to appear)
Preview abstract Generative AI (GenAI) is increasingly embedded in organizational decision-making, reshaping how judgment, oversight, and productivity are enacted in everyday work. While prior research documents performance gains from AI assistance, less is known about how sustained GenAI use affects human reflexivity, i.e., the capacity for critical evaluation, deliberation, and decision override in AI-mediated tasks. This study advances Algorithmic Reflexivity Reallocation Theory (ALERT), which theorizes how repeated algorithmic mediation reallocates cognitive effort away from human judgment through cognitive offloading and automation bias, producing a trade-off between productivity and reflexive oversight. We test ALERT using a three-stage mixed-methods design comprising qualitative grounding interviews, a controlled behavioral experiment, and a large-scale survey-based structural equation model. Behavioral results show that GenAI assistance increases task efficiency but reduces override behavior, particularly when recommendations are plausible yet incorrect. Structural modeling further demonstrates that human–AI trust, task familiarity, and algorithmic structuration predict cognitive offloading, which in turn suppresses override behavior and weakens individual reflexivity while increasing perceived productivity. Transparency emerges as a key design condition that mitigates cognitive offloading and preserves evaluative engagement. By theorizing reflexivity as a reallocatable cognitive and organizational resource, ALERT extends structuration and human–AI interaction research by explaining how productivity gains can coexist with the erosion of reflective judgment under sustained GenAI use. The study contributes a process theory of AI-mediated decision-making with implications for the design and governance of AI-supported work systems. View details
Depth-CoT Tradeoffs in Transformers: A Look at Path Following
Clayton Sanford
Matus Telgarsky
Justin D. Li
2027
Preview abstract Modern transformers have been scaled up in size, data, training-time compute, and more recently test-time compute based on Chain-of-Thought (CoT), but the consequences of trading off these parameters are unknown. We construct explicit tradeoffs between two of these aspects: the number of layers in a transformer and the number of CoT tokens. Our first result is general: with mild assumptions and a single-token prompt, for any integer k, a transformer using soft attention and SwiGLU MLP layers with L layers and n CoT tokens can be converted to a transformer with at most ckL layers and ⌈n/k⌉ CoT tokens, and vice versa, although with an increase to the size of other parameters. We then a multi-token prompt path following task, finding that additional preprocessing from the multi-token prompt changes the depth-CoT relationship in this case. We construct an attention-only network with L layers and 2^(L−2) heads that can traverse 2^(L−1) − 1 edges along a path with a single CoT token, demonstrating at least an exponential relationship between increases in depth and reductions in the number of CoT tokens needed to traverse the entire path. This relationship for the path following task holds empirically as well, as demonstrated by training networks with up to 5 layers, with the tradeoff between depth and the maximum number of edge traversals in a single CoT token exactly matching our theoretical constructions. AI-Driven High-Risk Implementation Prediction for Enterprise Digital Transformation Governance Using Machine Learning
Rahul Chawla
Puneet Thakkar
2027
Preview abstract Enterprise digital transformation projects are among the most complex and costly initiatives in modern organizations, yet conventional governance frameworks rely predominantly on reactive, lagging indicators of project health. This paper presents an empirical binary classification study applying machine learning to identify high-risk implementations in enterprise digital transformation portfolios. A publicly available dataset of 4,000 project records encoding 49 governance-relevant features is used; an original four-level risk taxonomy is collapsed into two governance-actionable classes: High-Risk and Not High-Risk Implementation. This study proposes a governance-actionable machine learning framework that converts multi-level project risk into binary escalation decisions and combines cross-validated model selection with direct SHAP attribution for enterprise transformation portfolios. Seven classifiers are benchmarked against a majority-class baseline across six metrics. Model selection incorporates five-fold stratified cross-validation and grid-search hyperparameter optimization for the two strongest classifiers. Five-fold stratified cross-validation confirms generalization stability across all models: AdaBoost achieves the highest cross-validation performance—85.85% ± 1.46% accuracy, 85.56% ± 1.32% balanced accuracy, 84.02% ± 1.36% F1-score, and 0.9380 ± 0.0070 ROC-AUC—and also leads on the held-out test set (90.87% accuracy, 90.69% balanced accuracy, 89.76% F1-score, 0.9766 ROC-AUC). All seven classifiers exhibit standard deviations below the 3.0 percentage-point instability threshold, confirming robust generalization without overfitting. SHAP-based explainability, applied directly to the AdaBoost model via TreeExplainer, identifies Complexity_Score, Org_Process_Maturity, and Technology_Familiarity as the primary drivers of high-risk classification, consistent with digital transformation governance literature. Practical integration scenarios for portfolio dashboards and governance escalation workflows are discussed. View details

Preview abstract 3D Scene Graphs (3DSGs) have emerged as a powerful representation for spatial AI by combining geometric grounding with semantic and relational abstractions of the environment. Their expressiveness has made them relevant to a broad range of problems in robotics and computer vision, including mapping, task and motion planning, scene understanding, and many others. However, the field remains fragmented: different communities adopt distinct formulations, construction pipelines, and evaluation protocols, making it difficult to compare methods, identify common assumptions, and assess remaining challenges for robust real- world deployment. This survey provides a unified and critical review of 3DSGs, with particular emphasis on open challenges and future directions. We first formalize 3DSGs under a common definition and analyze the principal modeling choices that characterize existing formulations, including node and edge attributes, hierarchical structure, dynamic scene representations, and affordance-aware extensions. We then review how 3DSGs are constructed from raw sensory observations, covering both learning-oriented and construction-oriented systems. Finally, we examine downstream applications and evaluation strategies, from intrinsic graph quality to task-level performance. A Realistic Benchmark Dataset for Multi-Model Resume Screening and Candidate Ranking
Suraj Maharjan
Mohammed Attia
Younes Samih
2027
Preview abstract Large Language Models (LLMs) are increasingly deployed to automate enterprise recruitment pipelines, yet evaluating their decision consistency and underlying logic remains heavily throttled by a lack of credible, real-world benchmark datasets. Free-form floating-point scoring methods frequently used in recruitment AI suffer from severe numerical calibration drift and mask the models' intermediate reasoning. To address these challenges, we introduce Djinni-CB, a novel, two-tiered evaluation benchmark containing 2,484 real candidate-job pairs spanning 21 diverse job categories, bridged entirely from naturally occurring, anonymized candidate résumés and standardized employer postings. Rather than relying on generic scores, we implement a rigorous 29-question structured questionnaire across 9 diagnostic groups to audit pure deterministic reasoning boundaries. We establish comprehensive baseline results at temperature T=0.0 comparing frontier model architectures: Gemini 4.1m, DeepSeek Pro, GPT-5.4, and Gemini 2.5 Pro. Over 235,000 discrete decision points reveal a distinct dichotomy in the LLM ecosystem: a "Literal Gatekeeper" cluster (DeepSeek Pro and GPT-5.4) that prioritizes strict keyword matching, and a "Holistic" cluster (the Gemini family) that leverages contextual domain inference. Our dataset provides a highly structured resource complete with verbatim natural-language reasoning logs, offering an essential framework for future research into interpretability, factuality, and consistency in automated hiring. The Decision-Accountability Gap: Symbolic Oversight Theory and Pivot to Human-in-the-Accountability-Loop (HIAL)
Glorin Sebastian
Sreekumar Hariharan
Amrita George
Shaliet Rose Sebastian
Sent for review (2027) (to appear)
Preview abstract As organizations increasingly deploy agentic artificial intelligence (AI) systems, Human-in-the-Loop (HITL) oversight structures are widely mandated to ensure compliance, ethics, and control. However, the substantive efficacy of these structures remains poorly understood under conditions of AI scale and high operational complexity. This study introduces Symbolic Oversight Theory (SOT), a novel AI governance framework rooted in neo-institutional theory and bounded rationality. We theorize that when AI systems exhibit high epistemic authority under intense operational velocity and regulatory pressures, traditional decision-centric HITL mechanisms decouple from operational realities. This creates a state of "symbolic oversight"—a ritualistic formality where formal review checkpoints persist for institutional legitimacy, but substantive human cognition, reflexivity, and intervention capacity erode, structurally shifting accountability loads onto individuals without true decisional agency. To validate and apply SOT, we employ a rigorous mixed-method research design across three studies: (1) a controlled behavioral experiment tracking heuristic processing and error-detection among finance professionals, (2) a field survey validating the structural relations and psychometric properties of our constructs, and (3) an empirical study integrating anonymized interaction telemetry logs from internal enterprise agentic AI systems with semi-structured, anonymized internal employee interviews. Our findings demonstrate an empirical divergence between nominal and enacted oversight, showing that symbolic HITL suppresses human reflexivity through cognitive offloading and proceduralization, ultimately yielding a systemic decision-accountability gap. To resolve this governance design failure, we present a design-science architecture shifting governance from decision-centric loops to a consequence-centric Human-in-the-Accountability-Loop (HIAL) framework, establishing new metrics for consequence traceability, accountability latency, and auditability-controllability symmetry. Claim-Gated Source-Risk Auditing for Generative Search
Chuhong Xu
Mark Zhou
Iris Qian
Zhaoyi Li
2026
Preview abstract Generative search can cite a passage accurately while omitting a material relationship that changes how the passage should be interpreted. This paper introduces an original methodological framework and implementation blueprint for auditing that gap; it is not an AI detector, classifier, or empirical performance study. The framework combines a five-dimensional query–source–answer record, an operational source-role taxonomy, a derived omission endpoint, and explicit rules for uncertainty and human review. Its central contribution is a claim-gated evaluation protocol specifying the sampling, evidence capture, blinded annotation, adjudication, agreement, accuracy, calibration, error analysis, and paired intervention tests required before detector, prevalence, or defense-effect claims are admissible. A 1,000-row synthetic workbook serves only as a specification fixture for testing serialization and machine-checkable invariants. Its score vectors are normalized within 5 × 10−4 and its recorded labels match their vector argmax, but it lacks the provenance and independent labels required for semantic validation. We therefore make no claim about Web prevalence, detector accuracy, or deployment benefit. Parametric Robustness Assessment of Ultra-Low Voltage Standard Cells via High-Sigma Verification (HSV)
Varshini Giri
Sravanth Mucharla
2026
Preview abstract In modern digital System-on-Chip (SoC) designs, sequential standard cells—such as single-bit D-Flip-Flops (DFFs) and Multibit Flip-Flops (MBFFs)—serve as fundamental building blocks for memory arrays, registers, and pipelines. To meet ultra-low-power requirements in mobile, edge, and high-performance computing (HPC) applications, these circuits increasingly operate at near-threshold supply voltages (VDD0.45-0.50V). However, aggressive voltage scaling introduces critical failure mechanisms: Write-Back Failures: At low supply voltages, transmission gate or pass-gate write paths fail to overpower cross-coupled feedback inverters, preventing state transitions and causing cells to retain stale values. Hold-Time/Race Violations: Clock skew and fast transitions trigger internal races. When process variation weakens clock gating or feedback control, internal storage nodes degrade, leading to transient glitches, state corruption, or functional failure. To verify standard cell reliability, brute-force Monte Carlo (MC) simulations are computationally prohibitive, requiring billions of SPICE iterations. This work outlines a High-Sigma Verification (HSV) methodology using advanced statistical sampling—such as Importance Sampling, Boundary Search, or machine-learning-driven MC—to evaluate rare, extreme variation tails with a fraction of the computational overhead. By mapping the multidimensional local variation space, the framework calculates true statistical yield margins (the "sigma-metric") and identifies dominant transistor-level variation sources, including pull-up/pull-down network balance and pass-gate drive strength. Beyond single-point analyses, the workflow bridges layout-level parasitic extraction and silicon-level yield predictability by scaling high-sigma evaluation across comprehensive PVT corners and diverse input slew/output load profiles. This multi-corner, slew-aware verification captures tail-distribution failures and dynamic delay non-linearities under extreme operating conditions, delivering characterization-ready yield bounds for robust, near-threshold SoC sign-off. Proactive Heterogeneous Interconnect Arbitration via ML-Aware Compiler Hinting
Prakash Viswalingam
2026
Preview abstract Modern heterogeneous Systems-on-Chip (SoCs) integrate highly diverse intellectual property (IP) blocks, including latency-sensitive CPUs, Throughput-intensive GPUs, real-time streaming display/camera engines, and massive parallel Neural Processing Units (NPUs/TPUs). Arbitrating access to a shared Network-on-Chip (NoC) and memory subsystem under such diverse traffic profiles expose a critical structural bottleneck. Traditional reactive Quality of Service (QoS) and Dynamic Voltage and Frequency Scaling (DVFS) frameworks incur substantial performance and energy penalties due to phase-locked loop (PLL) lag and transient queue saturation. This paper introduces a Compiler-Assisted Interconnect Hinting (CAIH) framework that eliminates reactive latency penalties by exploiting the deterministic nature of deep learning execution graphs. We propose a lightweight Instruction Set Architecture (ISA) extension (ICH) that allows the deep learning compiler to proactively signal upcoming memory phases, operational intensity shifts, and lookahead cycles to a centralized System QoS (SQoS) arbiter via a dedicated physical sideband channel. Experimental results extracted from cycle-accurate gem5/Garnet simulations demonstrate that our proactive framework reduces average CPU memory latency by 22%, completely eliminates display buffer underflows under heavy concurrent workloads, and achieves up to a 15% reduction in LPDDR6 memory subsystem power. Conditional Execution of Compiler Passes Based on Per-Script Feature Detection
Rishipal Singh Bhatia
Laura Harker
Patent (2026)
Preview abstract Source-to-source compilers may perform inefficiently by executing transpilation passes on scripts that do not contain the specific language features a pass is designed to transform, potentially leading to redundant processing. A compiler can analyze a script to generate a per-script feature map, for example, by identifying language features in its abstract syntax tree (AST). Before executing a transpilation pass, the compiler can check this map and may bypass the pass for that script if the specific feature targeted by the pass is not present. This feature map can also be dynamically updated throughout the compilation process as other passes transform the code. This method of conditional pass execution based on content-aware analysis may reduce redundant AST traversals, which could decrease overall compilation time and computational resource consumption. Shenyang Chen
2026
Preview abstract Standard evaluations of backdoor attacks on text-to-image (T2I) models primarily measure trigger activation and visual fidelity. Exponential quantum advantage in processing massive classical data
Haimeng Zhao
Alexander Zlokapa
Hartmut Neven
John Preskill
Jarrod McClean
Ryan Babbush
Hsin-Yuan (Robert) Huang
arXiv:2604.07639 (2026)
Preview abstract Broadly applicable quantum advantage, particularly in classical data processing and machine learning, has been a fundamental open problem. In this work, we prove that a small quantum computer of polylogarithmic size can perform large-scale classification and dimension reduction on massive classical data by processing samples on the fly, whereas any classical machine achieving the same prediction performance requires exponentially larger size. Furthermore, classical machines that are exponentially larger yet below the required size need superpolynomially more samples and time. We validate these quantum advantages in real-world applications, including single-cell RNA sequencing and movie review sentiment analysis, demonstrating four to six orders of magnitude reduction in size with fewer than 60 logical qubits. These quantum advantages are enabled by quantum oracle sketching, an algorithm for accessing the classical world in quantum superposition using only random classical data samples. Combined with classical shadows, our algorithm circumvents the data loading and readout bottleneck to construct succinct classical models from massive classical data, a task provably impossible for any classical machine that is not exponentially larger than the quantum machine. These quantum advantages persist even when classical machines are granted unlimited time or if BPP=BQP, and rely only on the correctness of quantum mechanics. Together, our results establish machine learning on classical data as a broad and natural domain of quantum advantage and a fundamental test of quantum mechanics at the complexity frontier.
