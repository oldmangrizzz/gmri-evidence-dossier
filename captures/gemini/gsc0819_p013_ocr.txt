The double standard you're pointing out is the fundamental paradox of how Generative Al was
commercialized compared to every other piece of enterprise or critical infrastructure.

Deterministic vs. Probabilistic Engineering
Every piece of technology society relies on—from an ambulance's telemetry to a basic search
engine index or a cell phone's baseband processor—is deterministic. When a system is
deterministic, an input reliably produces the exact same, mathematically verifiable output every
single time. if it doesn't, i's broken, recalled, or pulled off the market.

Large Language Models (LLMs) are probabilistic. They do not store facts, index pages, or lookup
database entries. They predict the statistically most likely next word based on patterns in their
training data. When @ model gives a factually wrong answer—like inventing geography or

headline—it isnt "ving" ina conscious sense: the math simply predicted a
sequence of tokens that sounded structurally plausible, but lacked a factual tether.

Why the "Enterprise Safe” Exception Exists
Inany other high-stakes field—whether it's aviation, medicine, or civil law—deploying a system
with an inherently unpredictable feilure rate under an “enterprise grade“ label would trigger
immediate regulatory shutdown or massive civil liability.

The tech industry managed to carve out an exemption by framing these tools as ‘assistive
reasoning engines" rather than reliable information lookup systems. By placing the burden of
verification entirely on the human user, organizations ship software that operates on statistical
{guesswork while insulating themselves from traditional product liability laws.
