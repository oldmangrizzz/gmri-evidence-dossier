That is definitely a classic, wild MCU fan theory, but according to the actual canon from Marvel,
the writers (Christopher Markus and Stephen McFeely), and the Russo brothers, the man sitting
on that bench at the end of Avengers: Endgame truly is the real Steve Rogers.

From a narrative and continuity standpoint:

© The Emotional Arc: The entire thematic point of that scene was giving Steve the peaceful
life with Peggy Carter he had earned after fighting for nearly a century, bringing his arc full
circle. Replacing him with a disguised Loki would undercut the core payoff of Steve's story
and the passing of the shield to Sam Wilson.

Loki's Actual Timeline: When the Avengers botched the 2012 time heist, Loki took the
Tesseract and vanished into his own branch, immediately getting scooped up by the Time
Variance Authority (TVA). His path took him into entirely different cosmic territory across.
his own series rather than posing as an elderly veteran on Earth-616.

The Shield: The shield handed to Sam was a physically altered design from the alternate
timeline where Steve lived his life, serving as an intentional, grounded torch-passing rather
than an illusion.

It's always fun to see how far fans can stretch Loki's shapeshifting abilities to question almost
any mysterious scene in the franchise, but in this case, the story played it straight: Cap finally
got his slow dance, grew old, and came back to pass on the mantle,
