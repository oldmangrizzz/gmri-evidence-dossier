Sep 14, 2026

Docket Text ORDER: In light of Plaintiff Ann Altman's failure to timely respond to Defendant Samuel Altman's Notice, to dispute his claim that she has invoked additional mental conditions, and to meaningfully explain why that development does not undermine her need for a protective order, see Docs. 100, 102, 103, the Court DENIES Annie's [92-1] request for a protective order limiting the scope of the IME. Moreover, even if PTSD were the only mental condition at issue in this case (which appears highly unlikely), the Court would have denied a protective order because Annie did not meet her burden to justify limiting the scope of an IME where a "mental condition is surely in controversy." See, e.g., Shannon v. Ellis, 2018 WL 4698783, at *1 (E.D. Mo. Oct. 1, 2018) ("The party seeking a protective order... has the burden to show good cause for it."); Aubuchon v. Tate Trucking, LLC, 2025 WL 3012997, at *2 (E.D. Mo. Oct. 28, 2025) ("[F]or the court to intervene and limit the type of examination an expert has indicated is necessary in order to analyze plaintiff's claims would subvert the truth finding function inherent in Rule 35 examinations."); Delvecchia v. Frontier Airlines, Inc., 2023 WL 1963162, at *2 (D. Nev. Feb. 13, 2023) (declining to limit the scope of an IME under similar circumstances). That said, Sam should instruct Dr. Hudson to not probe medical conditions "wholly unrelated to her lawsuit," see Aubuchon, 2025 WL 3012997, at *2, and nothing prevents Annie from seeking to limit how the information from the examination, either through a protective order during discovery or a motion in limine for purposes of trial. Signed by District Judge Zachary M. Bluestone on 09/14/2026. (JRZ) [modified as discussed at motion hearing on October 2, 2026 on 10/5/2026] (CLH). (Entered: 09/14/2026)
